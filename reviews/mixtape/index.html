Mixtape - 69/100

Review date: October 8, 2026, Author: Swiper4ik

An overhyped interactive movie with a confusing message.
Mixtape is a modern take on the '90s that merely pretends to be something wonderful. The graphics are far from groundbreaking, there is no gameplay challenge whatsoever, minigames repeat constantly, and the overarching story proves to be thoroughly average. If you rush through it without much thought, you might miss its flaws, but the second you engage your critical brain, every shortcoming becomes painfully obvious. It is clear that Mixtape desperately tries to stand out from the crowd, yet despite its pretensions, it falls well short of excellence.

The core narrative leaves a strange aftertaste. You control a typical '90s teenager alongside a crew of "unconventional" friends who spend their time listening to, rating, and curating cassette mixtapes for various occasions. Throughout the journey, you are subjected to the protagonist’s personal playlist—and it goes without saying that music taste is entirely subjective. The plot setup is straightforward: the group prepares for a wild night out before a move to New York, but Cassandra's father, a strict police officer, forbids her from attending the late-night party. The group discovers a secret hideout, but a sudden moment of indecision from Stacey sparks a conflict with Cassandra. Naturally, this is resolved quickly through mutual support, the father eventually accepts his daughter's growing maturity, and everything wraps up with a neat happy ending. Let’s be honest: this is a cliché, run-of-the-mill story with very little original flair.

The theme of generational divide and parental misunderstanding has been explored and dissected far better in numerous superior titles (ranging from Life is Strange to God of War Ragnarök). Here, the blend of '90s nostalgia, music tracks, and father-daughter conflict feels sterile and calculated—assembled specifically to trick the player into believing they are experiencing something unique. The ending of Mixtape is equally disappointing because it delivers almost no narrative payoff. While Cassandra reconciles with her father, none of the other characters undergo any meaningful growth or learn valuable lessons. Had these obstacles never occurred, the outcome would remain largely unchanged. The characters don't reach a satisfying emotional resolution; they simply survive a single event and move on with their lives.

Gameplay is the least interesting aspect of the package, yet it cannot be ignored entirely. On one hand, it leans heavily into the interactive movie genre, but on the other, it severely lacks mechanical depth. The gameplay is so thin and sparse that cutscenes feel like they occupy far more space than the actual play sessions and minigames combined. To be fair, the game does introduce a few novel moments—like surreal high jumps through the woods, escaping the police in a shopping cart, or controlling a drunk guy picking out cassettes in a store. However, everything else feels like a bad joke: slow walking segments, hunting for household items, and repetitive skateboard races lack punch and fail to engage. Granted, these mechanics function properly without glaring technical bugs, but only because the game deprives the player of free movement and any risk of failure. There is zero challenge: if you crash or get caught by the police, you simply respawn without losing any progress. If the developers wanted to accommodate casual players while keeping gaming purists engaged, a simple difficulty slider would have solved the problem instead of stripping the game of all stakes.

Visuals are stylized, yet noticeably unpolished. Shadow rendering struggles in several areas, which becomes especially apparent during night sequences. While cutscenes look decent, graphical shortcomings immediately surface the moment player control is restored.

Verdict:

A thoroughly mediocre title that might serve as an inoffensive one-time playthrough, but nothing more. If you turn off your critical brain and keep your expectations low, you might find some enjoyment here. However, for players seeking engaging gameplay mechanics or a genuinely deep narrative, Mixtape is an easy skip.
