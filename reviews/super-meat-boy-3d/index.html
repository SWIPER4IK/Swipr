Super Meat Boy 3D - 78/100

Review date: September 6, 2026, Author: Swiper4ik

A Great 3D Transition for Meat Boy.
Right from the initial teaser trailer, it was clear that the game successfully preserved the visual style, atmosphere, and distinct charm of its predecessors. However, the biggest concern naturally revolved around the gameplay transition—shifting a classically tight 2D precision platformer into full 3D could have easily ended in disaster. Fortunately, the execution turns out quite solid: it stands as a fully realized 3D platformer with smooth, responsive character movement where the abrupt dimensional leap feels surprisingly natural rather than jarring. That said, long-time fans of the franchise might still find themselves disappointed, as the title heavily inherits mechanics from Super Meat Boy Forever. Fresh content remains noticeably scarce, giving the impression that the developers struggled to originate new ideas and instead relied on a safe, commercially viable formula that lacks variety. Nevertheless, for newcomers to the series or casual fans of Meat Boy, the project delivers a rewarding experience—it runs flawlessly, making it a joy to push for better times and clear every stage.

Here, you will indeed encounter the exact same structural formula used in Super Meat Boy Forever. Once again, your primary goals involve defeating bosses, rescuing your family, and escaping visually striking, action-packed locations. This aspect is admittedly somewhat disappointing: when picking up a new entry in a franchise, you expect actual innovation, yet beyond the graphical overhaul and the spatial shift, there is very little genuinely new content on offer. While the game remains a pleasure to look at and offers a nostalgic return to this bizarre universe, the progression often feels like a case of Groundhog Day. On a positive note, the new boss encounters are a highlight, each featuring a distinct and highly creative thematic design. The visual presentation is exceptionally polished; while minor graphical hiccups do occur, they are far from critical. Bright daytime levels feel genuinely atmospheric, whereas the darker stages strike a nice balance by emphasizing high difficulty rather than pure horror. Furthermore, every death rewards you with the series' signature bloody explosion, which—thanks to the 3D rendering—now boasts impressive volume and fluid physics. The environments never feel empty or unfinished, as the level design maintains a relentless density of sawblades and lethal hazards.

Verdict:

An incredibly dynamic and stylish platformer that hooks you immediately, keeping you engaged even after dozens of failed attempts. You once again take control of Meat Boy, but this time in a whole new dimension. The gameplay feels genuinely satisfying, making every run enjoyable even in defeat. Furthermore, the visuals are thoroughly impressive—as expected from the series. Ultimately, it is slightly disappointing that the game didn't aim for a grander scale, deeper villain backstories, or fresh mechanics for its characters, but it remains a remarkably fun ride nonetheless.

The controls have not become overly complex; they remain just as intuitive as before, merely adapted into a full three-dimensional space. You move freely in all directions, jump, and slide down walls with ease. While it might initially seem like character movement would feel sluggish in 3D, the constantly ticking timer pushes you to keep a fast pace. As a result, the core gameplay loop feels virtually identical to Super Meat Boy Forever, since both games demand that you sprint toward the finish line along a tight, optimal trajectory. Crucially, the instant respawn mechanic remains intact, keeping frustration to a minimum after every fail. Additionally, the developers included a dedicated retry button, making it seamless to restart a stage immediately if you get off to a poor start.

The original soundtrack also deserves special mention, as it once again fits the gameplay style perfectly. The tracks inject a constant stream of adrenaline into the action, reinforcing the game's unrelenting sense of speed. That said, because the audio relies so heavily on a dense mix of Industrial Rock, Metal, and Chiptune, individual tracks tend to blend together and rarely stand out on their own—with the main menu theme being the sole memorable exception.
